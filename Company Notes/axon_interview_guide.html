🗺️ Full Interview Process at Axon

Reconstructed from recent (2025–2026) Senior Software Engineer candidate reports on Glassdoor, Blind, InterviewQuery and 1Point3Acres. The consistent recent pattern for senior backend roles: a recruiter screen, an early system-design screening, then a full loop of 2 coding + 1 system design + 1 behavioral, with at least one block onsite. Axon has been actively moving its coding rounds toward an AI-assisted format in 2026.

🛡️About Axon

Axon Enterprise (Nasdaq: AXON, formerly TASER International) builds a "public-safety operating system" for law enforcement, courts, corrections and enterprise security: TASER energy devices, Axon Body cameras, in-car/fixed cameras, drones & counter-drone (Dedrone), and a cloud software suite — Axon Evidence (digital evidence management), Axon Records, real-time operations, VR training, and AI tools like Draft One, Axon Assistant and Axon Vision. ~5,100 employees; FY2025 revenue ~$2.78B; Q1 2026 revenue $807M (up 34% YoY — its ninth straight quarter of 30%+ growth) with ARR of ~$1.5B.

  • Distributed systems built and operated in production at scale
  • Operability-first thinking ("what does this do at 3am?")
  • Strong opinions held with respect; commit-and-deliver
  • Cross-team influence without formal authority
  • Mentoring that levels up the whole team
  • Genuine alignment with the "Protect Life" mission
  • Comfort using agentic AI in day-to-day engineering

⚡ A Note on the 2026 AI-Assisted Coding Shift

Multiple recent candidates report Axon replacing or augmenting a traditional LeetCode round with a 90-minute AI-assisted coding round — roughly 45 minutes of building plus 45 minutes of extension and discussion — and at least one was told they could use their own VSCode / Claude Code setup. This is reinforced by the JD for the VR role, which explicitly asks for "experience leveraging agentic AI in all your day-to-day work." Treat AI fluency as a graded skill, not a crutch (see the Coding tab).

⚖️ Storage vs VR: Two Sr SWE II Roles, Two Very Different Mandates

Both roles are the same level (Sr Software Engineer II), same pay band ($148,500–$237,600), same 8+ years bar, and same hybrid schedule (onsite Tue–Fri, remote Mondays). The difference is what they're optimizing you for. Knowing this lets you steer every story toward what each team actually values.

⚡ The Core Difference in One Sentence

Storage (your call): A reliability & distributed-systems tech-lead role — keep petabyte-scale evidence safe, turn incident heroics into self-governing systems, and set engineering discipline across teams you don't own.
VR: A backend architecture & technical-direction role — build bullet-proof, low-latency cloud services for immersive training, lead design reviews, and help set the technical and organizational direction for a growing multi-site team.

🧠 How to Frame Yourself for Each

🔵 For Storage — Lead With Operability

  • "Here's a recurring incident class I eliminated in code, not with a runbook."
  • "I designed an invariant/contract so the next engineer couldn't get it wrong."
  • "I ran this system at scale — here's how it behaved under real failure."
  • "I drove a reliability/migration roadmap across teams I didn't manage."
  • "I grew engineers from 'works' to 'great software' — the team levels up, not just me."

🟣 For VR — Lead With Architecture & Direction

  • "I led architecture design reviews and set the bar for a team."
  • "I designed high-availability, low-latency services with hard SLAs."
  • "I made the right call among competing engineering trade-offs."
  • "I partner tightly with Product & Design to build the right thing."
  • "I've woven agentic AI into how I and my team ship day to day."

📋 Prep Checklist — Tailoring to the Two Roles

  • Pick your "home" story bank per role: For Storage, foreground a reliability/incident-elimination story. For VR, foreground an architecture-leadership story. Same career, different opening move.
  • Storage = distributed-systems depth: Be ready to go deep on durability, consistency, replication, data integrity, and how you reason about failure modes — not just feature delivery.
  • VR = stack specifics: Brush up Cassandra data modeling (partition keys, wide rows, tunable consistency) and a managed language you're strong in (C#/Java/Scala/Go).
  • Have one genuine failure/setback story ready: Recent Senior loops lean hard on "tell me about a mistake / what you'd do differently." A polished win-only narrative reads as evasive here.
  • Both want a multiplier: Every story should answer "how did this make the team better, not just the product?"
  • Connect to mission: Evidence integrity (Storage) and realistic training that keeps officers and the public safe (VR) both ladder up to "Protect Life." Say so, specifically.

📌 One Important Caveat

The Storage JD reads a notch above a typical Senior IC — "technical leadership of cross-team initiatives," carrying a banner, driving roadmaps across org boundaries. That's tech-lead / borderline-Staff framing inside a Senior II title. Lean into org-level impact there. The VR JD is a more classic senior-backend-with-architecture-leadership role. Calibrate how high you "zoom out" accordingly.

💬 Behavioral / Situational Round

Recent Senior SWE candidates describe a behavioral round that leans heavily on situational and "risk management" questions — examples of mistakes, conflict, and "what would you do differently." Axon's values (Be Obsessed, Aim Far, Win Right, Own It, Join Forces, Expect Candor) show up implicitly. Expect a conversational hiring-manager or senior-engineer interviewer who probes your specific decisions, not your team's.

🎯 How to Prepare

🔑 Axon-Specific Preparation Tips

  • Know the mission: "Protect Life." Be ready to articulate why public-safety tech matters to you — and engage thoughtfully with the fact that this is law-enforcement tooling (it's worth having a sincere, considered view).
  • Internalize the values: Be Obsessed, Aim Far, Win Right, Own It, Join Forces, Expect Candor. Weave them in naturally rather than reciting them.
  • Build a 6-story bank: teamwork, conflict, a real failure, ambiguity/ownership, mentoring/multiplier, and a reliability or architecture flagship. Each should flex to multiple questions.
  • Know every resume line: Interviewers dig into specific listed projects — be able to defend any bullet in depth.
  • Lead with business/operational outcome: open each story at the impact level (reliability, scale, cost, team velocity), then zoom into the technical detail.
✅Do
  • Own mistakes cleanly and show the systemic fix
  • Say "I decided…" — make your individual role unmistakable
  • Quantify reliability/scale/impact wherever possible
  • Tie technical leadership back to the "Protect Life" mission
  • Ask sharp, role-specific questions back
❌Avoid
  • Win-only narratives with no real failure
  • Vague "we" stories that hide your contribution
  • Harshly criticizing past employers
  • Dodging the "what would you do differently" follow-up
  • Generic answers with no Axon/role specificity

💻 Coding Rounds DSA Screen + AI-Assisted

Recent candidates describe two coding touchpoints: a traditional DSA screen (LeetCode easy–medium, language of choice — C# is common) and a newer 90-minute AI-assisted coding round (~45 min build + ~45 min extend & discuss), where you may be allowed to use your own VSCode / Claude Code setup. Onsite loops typically include two coding blocks. Focus is on correctness, clean code, and how you reason out loud.

📋 Format (Glassdoor, Blind, 1Point3Acres)

  • DSA screen: ~1 hr. Arrays, strings, hashmaps/sets, graph traversal, some DP. Mostly easy–medium; occasional hard.
  • AI-assisted round: 90 min total. First ~45 min building; second ~45 min extending, hardening, and discussing. May be a multi-file / scenario task rather than a pure puzzle.
  • Language: Your choice; C#/.NET shows up often given the stack. Java/Scala/Go all fine.
  • Platform: CoderPad-style shared editor; AI round may permit your own IDE + assistant.
  • Note: Some candidates report constraints like "no datetime libraries" — read the rules carefully.
📋 Example Questions (reported online — for pattern practice, not memorization)
🔤 Strings & Two-Pointer
Valid Palindrome II · Longest Palindromic Substring. Classic string/two-pointer and expand-around-center patterns.
💡 Reported repeatedly across Axon coding rounds. Get fluent at in-place string scanning and the edge cases (empty, single char, all-same).
🔢 Binary Search on Answer
Capacity To Ship Packages Within D Days — minimize a threshold via binary search over the feasible range.
💡 Know the "binary search on the answer" template cold; interviewers like to see you recognize it from a non-obvious prompt.
📅 Implementation w/ Constraints
"Day of the Week" — compute the weekday from a date without using any date/time libraries (Zeller-style logic).
💡 Tests careful implementation and handling artificial constraints. Clarify the constraint, then narrate your math.
🧩 Practical / Design-flavored
Reported prompts: a "currency conversion" problem, a "GPS recorder," and "design a data structure." More applied than pure-puzzle.
💡 For these, start with the interface/contract and test cases before the algorithm — it signals the operability mindset Axon prizes.
🌐 Graphs & DP (onsite)
Onsite candidates report graph traversals (BFS/DFS) and DP (including a matrix-chain-style problem). "LeetCode-tagged questions are not always sufficient."
💡 Don't only grind the Axon tag — cover core graph + DP patterns broadly so a novel prompt doesn't surprise you.

🔑 Preparation Tips — Including the AI-Assisted Round

  • Drill the core patterns: two pointers, sliding window, hashmap counting, binary search on answer, BFS/DFS, and a handful of DP shapes. Easy–medium fluency > one hard solve.
  • Talk while you code: Axon values seeing your reasoning and trade-offs as much as the final answer.
  • For the AI round, optimize for the transcript: these sessions are often replayed. Tight, intentional prompts beat a messy "make it work" loop. State what you expect the AI to return and how you'll verify it.
  • Show "agent shepherding": scope tasks for the assistant, review its output critically, run the tests yourself, and intervene when it heads down a bad path. The skill being graded is judgment, not delegation.
  • Verify, don't trust: write/run tests, check edge cases by hand, and call out anything the AI got subtly wrong — that's exactly the signal Axon wants per the VR JD's "agentic AI in your day-to-day."
  • Pick your language deliberately: default to your strongest; mention C#/.NET familiarity if you have it, since it maps to the stack.

🏗️ System Design Round

Senior loops include an early system-design screening plus a design block in the onsite. Expect API design, data modeling, scalability and high-availability tradeoffs — and for these two roles, a strong bias toward distributed-systems reliability (Storage) and low-latency, bullet-proof cloud services (VR). Interviewers care about how you approach the problem and reason about trade-offs, not a single "right" diagram. One candidate flagged a biased/opinionated interviewer — stay collaborative and probe assumptions.

📋 Format (Glassdoor & candidate reports)

  • Covers: API design, schema/data modeling, scaling, availability, failure modes, consistency
  • Stack context: SQL + Cassandra, Azure and/or AWS, microservices
  • Two interviewers may join; collaborative and conversational
  • Axon-specific constraints reward you: petabyte scale, data integrity, audit/chain-of-custody, and CJIS-style compliance for criminal-justice data
📋 Example / Likely Design Prompts
🗄️ Petabyte Evidence Storage (Storage role)
Design a system to store and serve petabyte-scale digital evidence (body-cam video) durably, with guaranteed integrity and fast retrieval.
💡 Hit: tiered/object storage (hot vs cold), erasure coding/replication for durability, checksums + integrity verification, immutability & retention policies, metadata store, and a self-healing path for bit-rot. This is the role's core — go deep.
📥 High-Throughput Ingest Pipeline
Design the upload/ingest pipeline for thousands of officers uploading large video files, with resumable uploads and back-pressure.
💡 Cover: chunked/resumable uploads, queue + async processing, idempotency, transcoding, and graceful degradation under a shift-change spike. Tie reliability to the JD's "self-governing systems."
⚡ Low-Latency Service (VR role)
Design a highly-available, low-latency cloud service backing a real-time/immersive feature, with strict latency SLAs.
💡 Cover: read/write paths, caching, Cassandra data modeling (partitioning, tunable consistency), regional failover, and how you'd measure/enforce p99 latency. Maps to VR's "bullet-proof" framing.
🔒 Chain-of-Custody & Compliance
How do you guarantee tamper-evidence, audit trails, access control and data residency for criminal-justice evidence?
💡 Talk immutable audit logs, cryptographic hashing/signing, RBAC, encryption at rest/in transit, and CJIS-style data-handling requirements. Few candidates surface compliance — it's memorable here.
🔌 API & Invariants
Design the API and data contracts for an evidence service such that the next engineer "can't get it wrong."
💡 Lead with interface contracts and invariant checks enforced in code — the exact phrasing in the Storage JD. Show how the design routes errors to software, not humans.

🔑 Preparation Tips

  • Start from requirements & failure modes: clarify scale, durability target, consistency needs, and what "3am behavior" must be — before drawing boxes.
  • Make reliability the spine of your answer: for Storage, durability/integrity/operability should drive the design, not be a footnote.
  • Know Cassandra trade-offs: partition design, write-optimized paths, tunable consistency, and when not to use it vs SQL.
  • Talk trade-offs explicitly: SQL vs Cassandra, sync vs async, replication vs erasure coding, strong vs eventual consistency — reasoning beats a single answer.
  • Sketch it: use a shared whiteboard / Excalidraw; narrate as you draw.
  • Cloud familiarity helps: Azure and AWS both appear; S3/Blob, queues, and managed DBs are fair game.

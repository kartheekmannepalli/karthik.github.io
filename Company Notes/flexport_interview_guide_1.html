🚢About Flexport

Founded in 2013 by Ryan Petersen, Flexport is a technology platform for global logistics — freight forwarding across ocean, air, and trucking, plus customs, fulfillment, and trade finance. It moves $19B+ of merchandise across 112 countries a year in a ~$10T industry. After acquiring Convoy (trucking) and Shopify Logistics, the company went "code red" on AI in 2025 and shipped 20+ AI products. This role sits on a brand-new AI-first team building the autonomous rates & booking layer.

  • Agentic orchestration — Temporal, reliable systems around non-deterministic AI
  • Entrepreneurial drive — find the highest-leverage automation, no spec needed
  • Architecture leadership across multiple teams/systems
  • Product instincts — decide what's worth building
  • Technical communication to business partners & PMs

🚀 Senior → Staff Engineer: What Changes

This is not just a bigger Senior role. The jump to Staff is a fundamentally different way of operating — from execution to strategy and multiplication. At Flexport, this Staff role is the technical anchor of a brand-new team and codebase, so leveling signal matters even more than usual.

⚡ The Core Shift in One Sentence

Senior Engineer: Optimized for fast, high-quality delivery within your team.
Staff Engineer: Optimized for multiplication — enabling others, shaping technical direction, and driving org-wide strategic impact. You're judged not just by what you ship, but by how you raise the floor for everyone around you.

🧠 Mindset Comparison

🔵 Senior Engineer Thinking

  • "How do I implement this agent correctly?"
  • "How do I improve my team's velocity?"
  • "What's the best technical approach for this problem?"
  • "How can I mentor my junior teammates?"
  • "I need to ship this on time and with quality."

🟣 Staff Engineer Thinking

  • "Should we build this agent — or is the leverage elsewhere?"
  • "How do I set patterns that make the whole new team faster?"
  • "What makes a non-deterministic system reliable enough to ship to clients with no human in the loop?"
  • "How do I grow a senior engineer toward staff?"
  • "What technical strategy moves Flexport from assisted-sales to tech-run?"
🎯 What to Keep in Mind During Your Staff Interview at Flexport
🔑 Lead with Impact, Not Implementation
Start every story at the strategic level — business or org impact — before the technical detail. For an AI-first team, anchor on the outcome the automation unlocked (revenue, ops hours saved, conversion).
💡 Common mistake: diving straight into model/framework details. Zoom out first ("this removed the account-executive touch for X% of bookings"), then zoom in.
✖️ Show Yourself as a Multiplier
For every story answer: "How did this make the people around me better or faster?" On a greenfield team, the patterns and infra you set are the multiplier.
💡 e.g. "I built the eval harness + prompt-versioning workflow the whole team now uses" or "my agent-reliability runbook cut our incident triage time."
🌐 Demonstrate Cross-Functional Influence
Bring examples of aligning engineering with product, pricing/finance, ops, and design — Flexport explicitly wants you partnering with Pricing, Sales, Ops & Design.
💡 The JD calls out "convey technical ideas in common terms to business partners." Show you translate, not just build.
🗺️ Speak to Technical Strategy, Not Just Execution
When discussing design, zoom out: why does this agent architecture matter for Flexport's long-term move to a tech-run model? What are the org-level tradeoffs?
💡 Think in years: how does today's orchestration choice scale across ocean, air, and trucking pricing?
🌫️ Prove You Can Navigate Ambiguity
Have a story where you created structure from nothing — no spec, no owner. The JD literally says you'll solve problems that "don't have Stack Overflow answers yet."
💡 If your stories always start with "we were given a clear requirement," they read as senior, not staff.

📋 Interview Preparation Checklist — Staff Level

  • Reframe your stories: upgrade STAR stories with the business-impact layer, cross-team influence, and org-level outcome.
  • Prepare 2–3 "defining project" stories that show you led something ambiguous, cross-functional, and high-stakes end to end — at least one should be AI/agent-related.
  • Know your "multiplier" moments: times you made other engineers meaningfully faster or more independent.
  • Be ready for "tell me about your technical vision": how you'd shape the rates/booking platform's direction.
  • Don't default to "we": interviewers probe for your specific decisions and leadership.
  • Connect everything to Flexport's mission: making global trade easy/accessible, and the shift from assisted-sales to tech-run.

🤖 AI Project Deep Dive Role-Specific Round

This round is unique to the Autonomous Freight Systems team and is confirmed in the job posting. The application asks you to describe a project where you applied RAG or agentic workflows to a real-world business problem, and which orchestration layer you used. Expect the live round to go deep on that same story: architecture, reliability, evaluation, and the business outcome.

📋 The exact prompt you're being measured against

"Briefly describe a project where you successfully applied RAG or agentic workflows to solve a real-world business problem. What was the orchestration layer you used?"

Have one story that hits all four beats: (1) the business problem, (2) the agentic/RAG approach, (3) the orchestration layer by name, (4) the measurable result. Then be ready to defend every layer of it.

📋 What they'll likely probe (mapped to the JD)
🧠 Agent Patterns & RAG in Production
"Walk me through your agent/RAG architecture. What worked in production vs. what only looked good in a demo?"
💡 The JD literally says "you know what works in production versus what only looks good in a demo." Have a concrete example of a demo-stage approach you had to harden — chunking strategy, retrieval quality, grounding, hallucination guards.
⚙️ Orchestration & Reliability
"How did you build a reliable system around a non-deterministic model? What was your orchestration layer and why?"
💡 Temporal is named in the JD. Be ready to discuss retries, idempotency, human-in-the-loop fallback, timeouts, compensation/rollback, and durable execution for long-running agent workflows.
🔐 Safely Exposing Internal Data to LLMs
"How would you let an LLM use sensitive internal logistics/pricing data without leaking it or acting on bad data?"
💡 JD: "secure AI infrastructure required to expose internal logistics data to LLMs safely." Discuss access scoping, PII handling, prompt-injection defenses, tool/function calling with guardrails, and audit trails.
📊 Evaluation & Quality Bar
"How did you measure whether the agent was good enough to remove the human? How did you catch regressions?"
💡 Talk about eval datasets, offline/online evals, golden sets, LLM-as-judge with caveats, confidence thresholds, and gating autonomy by confidence — directly relevant to "commit freight on technology alone."
💸 Business Framing
"Why was this the right problem to automate? What did it unlock for the business?"
💡 Tie it to leverage: ops hours removed, faster quoting, self-serve conversion, error reduction. This connects straight to "move from assisted-sales to tech-run."

🔑 Preparation Tips

  • Pick one production story, not three shallow ones. Depth beats breadth here — they will follow up relentlessly on the one you choose.
  • Name your orchestration layer and defend it (Temporal, LangGraph, Airflow, custom state machine, step functions). Know its failure modes.
  • Rehearse the "demo vs. production" contrast — it's a stated evaluation criterion.
  • Be honest about limitations. Staff signal includes knowing where your approach breaks and how you'd mitigate. Avoid overclaiming model capabilities.
  • Map your story to freight/rates if you can — parsing rate sheets, pricing intelligence, booking compliance are the actual problems on this team.
  • If your strongest AI work is internal/early-stage, frame it outcome-first so the interviewer doesn't need framework-level context to see the value.
🙋 5 Questions to Ask — AI Deep Dive Round

Your interviewer is likely a founding member of this AI-first team. These questions show you think about agent systems at a staff level and are evaluating whether the team can actually ship autonomy.

Question 1 · Autonomy Bar
"What's the bar for letting an agent commit freight with no human in the loop — how do you decide when a workflow is reliable enough to go fully autonomous?"
💡 Goes straight at the team's hardest product question and shows you think about confidence-gating and risk, not just capability.
Question 2 · Orchestration Choices
"Where has the team landed on orchestration — Temporal or otherwise — and what have been the painful lessons building durable, reliable workflows around non-deterministic models?"
💡 Signals hands-on familiarity with the exact infra problem and invites a candid technical exchange.
Question 3 · Eval Infrastructure
"How mature is the evaluation infrastructure today? Is there a golden dataset and regression suite for the rate-parsing and pricing agents, or is that still being built?"
💡 Evals are the unglamorous backbone of shippable AI — asking shows you know where the real work is.
Question 4 · Data Safety
"What does the secure layer for exposing internal pricing/logistics data to LLMs look like right now — and where would a Staff engineer have the most leverage to harden it?"
💡 Directly references a JD responsibility and scopes the actual greenfield work you'd own.
Question 5 · Build vs. Buy
"How does the team decide build vs. buy for the AI stack — foundation models, agent frameworks, vector stores — and how often is that revisited as the landscape moves?"
💡 Staff-level strategic framing; also tells you how much architectural ownership you'd genuinely have.
💻 Coding Round

Flexport coding interviews skew toward LeetCode easy–medium plus object-oriented / low-level design (LLD), often in a "build-on" format where the interviewer extends requirements as you go. You'll work in a shared editor while narrating. Reviewers describe the bar as not algorithmically brutal, but they expect clean, near-complete implementations because role competition is high.

📋 Format (from Glassdoor, LeetCode Discuss & Prepfully)

  • Style: 1–2 problems per round; frequently a DSA problem that morphs into an OOD/LLD design (e.g., "now model this as classes").
  • Difficulty: Mostly LC easy–medium. Some SDE-2/India loops report a 4-question, 90-min HackerRank screen of medium–hard.
  • Language: Your choice; this role's stack is Java / Ruby on Rails / React. The online assessment can include front-end and SQL questions.
  • Platform: Shared code editor (CoderPad / HackerRank style).
  • Watch-out: Some interviewers are quiet/disengaged — keep narrating and drive structure yourself.
📋 Example Questions (reported online)
🎮 OOD / LLD — "Build-On" (Most Common)
Design a class for a game player / model a game's entities — then the interviewer adds requirements incrementally. One reported variant: "I only wanted the Chess Piece class" after a broad start.
💡 Confirm the exact scope before designing broadly. Start with the smallest correct class, then extend. Reported on multiple Glassdoor + LeetCode reviews.
🪙 Token / Pricing Logic
"Design a function in a gaming app to buy entities using tokens, where entities have different prices that are combinations of different tokens."
💡 Maps neatly onto Flexport's domain (pricing combinations). Clarify edge cases — insufficient tokens, multiple valid combinations — before coding.
🔁 Recursion / DFS
A pure DFS problem and a recursion-heavy problem, where follow-ups make it progressively harder and you're expected to pass all test cases.
💡 Practice recursion → iterative conversions and clean base-case handling. They escalate, so leave room to extend.
🔤 String / Grid Manipulation
String parsing/transformation tasks, and simple grid generation like "implement a checkerboard."
💡 Nothing exotic — but clean, readable, bug-free code is the differentiator. Write small helpers and test as you go.
🗃️ Data Structures + SQL/Front-end (Assessment)
Arrays, hashmaps, sets at easy–medium; the online assessment may also include SQL and front-end questions.
💡 For this full-stack-leaning role, brush up on a SQL join/aggregation and a small React component in case the screen includes them.

🔑 Preparation Tips

  • Drill LLD/OOD: parking lot, deck of cards, chess pieces, vending machine, rate/pricing engine — practice starting minimal and extending cleanly.
  • Clarify scope first: the build-on format punishes over-engineering early. Ask "what exactly do you want first?"
  • Narrate constantly: with quiet interviewers, your spoken reasoning is most of the signal.
  • Write production-quality code: naming, small functions, edge cases, a quick test — they reward "easy to extend, hard to break."
  • Have SQL + a React snippet warm given the front-end/data nature of the team.

💡 Connects the JD's velocity claim to real engineering discipline — a staff-level reliability concern.

💡 Tells you how much real leverage you'd have and how the team scales.
🏗️ System Design Round

Flexport's design round spans high-level architecture and data-model design for real-world business systems, and often includes object/class design too. Interviewers care about how you approach the problem — clarifying requirements, modeling entities, discussing tradeoffs, and scaling — more than a single "right" answer. For this role, expect AI-agent and rates-platform flavored prompts.

📋 Format (from Glassdoor, LeetCode Discuss & System Design Handbook)

  • Covers: API design, database schema modeling, scalability, fault tolerance, and increasingly AI/agent architecture
  • Strong recurring pattern: "design a database schema for a real-world business" and entity modeling
  • Often collaborative and conversational; sometimes two interviewers
  • Recommended structure: clarify → model entities → data flow/ingestion → storage → scaling/tradeoffs → summary
  • Designing Data-Intensive Applications is the most-cited prep reference among candidates
📋 Example Questions (reported online + role-adapted)
🔔 Notification System (Reported, SDE-2)
"Design a notification system. It must be SaaS / multi-tenant and support multiple notification modes (email, SMS, push, in-app)."
💡 Cover fan-out, templating, delivery guarantees, retries/dedup, rate limiting, and per-tenant config. A genuine past Flexport HLD prompt.
📦 Shipment / Freight Tracking
"Design a shipment tracking system across air, sea, and ground. Handle high-volume tracking events."
💡 Model shipment, carrier, tracking event, user. Ingest via Kafka/Kinesis; discuss real-time vs. batch, idempotent event processing, and read scaling. Directly on-domain.
🗄️ Database Schema for a Real-World Business
"Design the schema for a booking / quoting system (clients, rates, quotes, bookings, carriers, payments)."
💡 The most consistently reported Flexport design prompt. Show relationships, indexing, how you'd version rates over time, and prevent double-booking with concurrency control.
🤝 Unified Rates Aggregation (Role-Specific)
"Design a service that aggregates pricing from multiple backend systems (ocean, air, trucking) into one client-facing rates view."
💡 The JD calls out "unified interfaces that aggregate data from various backend systems." Discuss caching, freshness/SLA per source, partial failures, and consistency tradeoffs.
🤖 Autonomous Booking Agent (Role-Specific)
"Design the system that lets an AI agent parse a rate sheet and auto-create a compliant booking with no human touch."
💡 Bring in orchestration (Temporal), retrieval/RAG, validation & compliance checks, confidence thresholds with human fallback, audit logging, and idempotency. This is the round where AI + classic design meet.

🔑 Preparation Tips

  • Always clarify first: shipment types, scale, latency needs, consistency requirements. Flexport interviewers reward scoping questions.
  • Lead with the data model: their prompts lean heavily on schema/entity design — get the entities and relationships right early.
  • Narrate tradeoffs out loud: "I considered WebSockets but events arrive irregularly, so a queue + polling simplifies retries." Thinking aloud is explicitly valued.
  • Sketch it: use a shared whiteboard/Excalidraw even on video. Diagrams > prose.
  • Know AWS + messaging: S3, RDS/Aurora, SQS/Kinesis, plus Temporal for durable workflows.
  • For AI prompts, separate the deterministic spine from the non-deterministic model — design for failure of the model, not just the infra.
